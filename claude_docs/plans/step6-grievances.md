# Step 6: grievances and ultimatums (plan, 2026-10-15)

No war out of nowhere: a computer nation that wants war first
complains, then sets terms, and only then declares war.

## The three stages

1. **Complaint** (3 turns): "France complains: your soldiers stand near
   our colonies." Turn report; shown on their nation card.
2. **Ultimatum** (3 turns): "France demands 500 gold, or war." A
   dialog for the player (Accept = pay). A computer nation pays when it
   can and fears the other side, else refuses.
3. **War**, if refused or ignored. Paying settles it: no new grievance
   against that nation for 15 turns, and they remember the kept word.

If the reason for war goes away, an open grievance is dropped after
12 turns.

## Causes (what they can see)

In this order: your soldiers within 2 tiles of their colonies; harm
they remember (lost units, a colony taken, a broken word); your
colonies within 4 tiles of theirs; else "you stand in the way of our
plans" (their goals).

## The demand

A quarter of the other side's gold (the Foreign Affairs report shows
it), rounded to 50, between 200 and 2,000.

## Which wars

Every war a computer nation declares by its own choice: against the
player (the old tension rule) and against computer nations (the
diplomat). Not: calls to arms, joint wars, coalition wars (they have
a public reason already), the King's wars, the tribes.

## Paying a tribe to attack another tribe

- The player: the missionary's "incite" now also lists the other
  tribes you have met. The price is lower against the tribe's old
  rival.
- A computer nation at war with a tribe pays a tribe that dislikes
  it (its old rival first) to go to war with it.
- A tribe that hates another (tension at the top) goes to war, also
  when it is not its old rival.

## Seen

Nation cards and the Diplomacy table: "Complaint: your soldiers near
our colonies" / "Ultimatum: 500 gold, 2 turns left". The Offers list
shows ultimatums and the tribes' offers too.
